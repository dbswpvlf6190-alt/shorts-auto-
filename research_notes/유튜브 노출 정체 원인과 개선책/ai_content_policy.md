# YouTube AI-generated / mass-produced / TTS content: 2025–2026 policy and how it affects distribution

Scope note: researched 2026-11-28. The help-center pages were fetched live that day. Where a claim comes only from blogs or vendor sites, it is marked as such. Our case is a Korean faceless real-estate tax and pension channel: FLUX images, Supertonic TTS, daily templated Shorts, long-form twice a week, containsSyntheticMedia=true, not in YPP (253 subs).

## Q1. What do the July 2025 "inauthentic content" update and the 2026 changes actually say? Do they affect non-monetized channels' recommendations?

### Takeaway
"Inauthentic content" is a YouTube Partner Program (YPP) monetization-eligibility rule. It is not a recommendation rule.
- **July 15, 2025:** "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content" and clarified to cover mass-produced and templated content.
- **July 2026:** the guidance was reorganized into three headings: Generic or Repetitive / Unsatisfying or Off-putting / AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics.

Neither text mentions TTS or recommendations. A channel outside YPP is not directly "penalized" by it. Separately, YouTube's CEO says the company uses its spam and clickbait systems to "reduce the spread" of low-quality, repetitive AI content. That second, distribution-level mechanism is what could matter to us.

### Cited Findings
- **Live YPP policy page changelog (fetched 2026-11-28):** the latest dated entry is July 15, 2025. It says the update clarified the "repetitious content" policy to cover repetitive or mass-produced content, and renamed it: "We are also renaming this policy from 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content.'" There is no separately dated 2026 entry, although the page body now contains the 2026 three-heading structure. — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **"Generic or Repetitive Content" (live text):**
  - Definition: "content that looks like it's made with a template."
  - Not allowed: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value." Also not allowed: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production."
  - Allowed: the "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different," and a series where each video has a distinct focus.
  - Key principle: "the substance of each video should be materially varied and deliver creative, educational, or other value." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **"Reused content":** not allowed is "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **"Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content":**
  - Definition: content that "relies heavily on emotionally manipulative formulas," mimics formats so videos feel interchangeable, or aims to shock for views.
  - Not allowed: AI clips stitched together without logical progression, and deceptive imagery such as fake disasters.
  - Explicitly allowed: "AI used to build unique characters, narratives, scripts, or backgrounds." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **"AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics":**
  - Covers channels using AI-generated personas that present themselves as human experts advising on health, legal, financial, or political topics.
  - Examples: "AI-generated podcast hosts offering financial guidance, investment tips, or wealth management advice" and "AI personas giving legal advice or interpreting laws."
  - Rule: "channels uploading this content will not be allowed to monetize." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Channel-level scope:** the page says "these guidelines apply not only to individual videos, but also to your channel overall," and the reused content policy "applies to your channel as a whole." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Tubefilter (2026-07-13)** calls the July 2026 rewrite "a communications fix" with "clearer, more actionable language." It says the rules did not change. The "unsatisfying or off-putting" category is a new label for content that was already ineligible. The article does not address recommendations. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)
- **YouTube's framing of the 2025 change:**
  - YouTube called it a "minor update" to long-standing YPP policy. Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie said the main change was the rename, and that it was not a crackdown on reaction or commentary channels. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows)
  - Ritchie: "This is to clarify that the policy includes content that's mass produced or repetitive, which is content viewers often consider spam." YouTube directed NPR to his video for details. — [NPR, 2025-08-28](https://www.npr.org/2025/08/28/nx-s1-5493485/ai-slop-videos-youtube-tiktok)
- **YouTube's examples of mass-produced content (2025):** narrated-story channels with only superficial differences between videos, and slideshow channels that all share the same narration. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/); [Tubefilter 2025-07-03](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/03/youtube-crackdown-repetitive-non-original-content/amp/)
- **Korean press (Newsis, 2026-07-26):**
  - Excluded from ad revenue: near-identical AI/CGI mass production where only parts of the visuals, audio, or text change; sensational clip compilations; content made to provoke anxiety or disgust; and AI avatars imitating a real person giving finance, medical, or legal information.
  - It says "AI 사용 자체를 제한하는 게 아닌" (AI use itself is not restricted). The test is whether the creator's judgment and originality shaped the video.
  - It paraphrases YouTube Trust & Safety's Matt Halprin (Creator Insider): viewers feel uncomfortable with such content and may stop using YouTube.
  - It does not say compliant content loses reach. — [Newsis](https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISX20260726_0003723647); see also [SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/amp/article/20000324642), [ITWorld Korea](https://www.itworld.co.kr/article/4020640/), [Newneek](https://newneek.co/@newneek/article/33898)
- **Mohan's 2026 letter (2026-01-21) states a distribution goal, separate from monetization:** "To reduce the spread of low quality AI content, we're actively building on our established systems that have been very successful in combatting spam and clickbait, and reducing the spread of low quality, repetitive content." The same letter says "AI will remain a tool for expression, not a replacement." — [YouTube Blog: The Future of YouTube 2026](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026/)
- **January 2026 enforcement:** a spokesperson pointed to existing spam and deceptive-practices rules, not a new policy, when large AI-slop channels were removed. — [NoFaceTube](https://nofacetube.com/rules/youtube-ai-slop-channel-removals/)
  - Reported figures (16 channels, 35M subscribers, 4.7B views) trace back to Outlierkit, and the two tellings conflict (11 terminated + 5 wiped). They are not confirmed by YouTube. — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320629/20260715/youtube-wiped-35m-subscribers-over-ai-slop-now-its-judging-your-taste.htm); [Outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)
- **Strikes vs monetization:** inauthentic content is a monetization rule, and strikes or terminations come from the Spam policy. This comes from a blog explainer, not official text. — [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/); [channelterminated.com](https://channelterminated.com/demonetized/inauthentic-content/)

### Inferences
- **Direct effect on us:** for a non-YPP channel, the inauthentic-content text has none today. It only decides whether we can monetize when we apply at 500 or 1,000 subscribers, and that review is channel-wide. Daily Shorts from one template (same layout, same voice, same card style, "~해요" script skeleton) are the pattern the "Generic or Repetitive" heading describes. They could cause a YPP rejection even if long-form is fine.
- **The AI Personas rule probably does not hit us** as long as the TTS narrator is not presented as a named human tax expert, advisor, or "세무사." It would matter if we invented an AI host character giving tax advice. Our content interprets tax law ("interpreting laws" is a listed example), so keep the narrator a neutral explainer voice, not a fake expert persona.
- **Distribution:** the real lever is the spam/low-quality systems Mohan describes. Those apply to every channel, monetized or not, but YouTube publishes no criteria. Whether our channel is caught by them cannot be verified from outside.

### Gaps
- The help page has no 2026 changelog entry, so the exact effective date of the three-heading restructure cannot be confirmed from YouTube. Blogs say July 16, 2026; Tubefilter dates it ~July 13, 2026.
- No official text says how, or whether, "low quality, repetitive content" is detected for recommendation demotion, or whether it is the same classifier used for YPP review.
- No official statement on TTS specifically. The term does not appear in the policy page.

## Q2. Is there evidence that AI-voice / AI-image faceless channels get throttled in Shorts or Browse? What has YouTube said about "AI slop" and recommendation quality?

### Takeaway
There is no official statement and no credible dataset showing that AI-voice or AI-image channels are systematically throttled because they use AI. YouTube's stated position is the opposite: AI is fine and quality decides distribution.
- YouTube is actively building signals against "slop": spam/clickbait-system extensions, and a viewer survey asking whether a video "feels like AI slop."
- Content that viewers perceive as slop is plausibly demoted through satisfaction signals, not through an "AI" flag.
- Evidence that faceless channels in general are hit is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube's recommendation inputs:** the system uses satisfaction surveys, "Don't recommend channel" feedback, and watch history. It says channel reputation and quality play a role, and that external evaluators assess how an average viewer perceives content. — [How YouTube recommendations work (Help)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en); [How YouTube Works: Recommendations](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/recommendations/)
- **"Valued watchtime":** YouTube measures time spent on videos viewers rate 4–5 stars in surveys. — [YouTube Blog: On YouTube's recommendation system](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- **"AI slop" survey (since about March 2026):** some mobile users get a pop-up asking how much a video feels like low-quality AI content ("Not at all" to "Extremely"). YouTube has not officially documented how answers affect recommendations. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/youtube-is-now-asking-viewers-if-videos-feel-like-ai-slop/); [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/youtube-prompt-asking-does-this-feel-like-ai-slop); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/YouTube-now-asks-viewers-to-detect-generative-AI-slop-when-rating-videos.1253227.0.html)
- **The Next Web (2026-06-15)** reports faceless creators as "collateral damage." Its evidence:
  - One creator (Doctor NOS, 1.7M subs) told The Hollywood Reporter that faceless peers are being demonetized.
  - Some faceless creators reportedly hire cheap on-camera hosts. Niche educational faceless channels reportedly held up better than broad ones.
  - It quotes YouTube: "AI-labelled videos will not be penalised in recommendations or lose access to monetisation."
  - The article itself gives no reach data. Its claims are anecdotal. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- **Kapwing (Nov 2025):**
  - In a fresh account, 21% (104) of the first 500 Shorts were AI-generated and 33% were "brainrot."
  - 278 of about 15,000 top channels post only AI slop, with 63B views combined.
  - So AI content clearly gets massive Shorts distribution. It is not throttled as a class. — [Kapwing AI Slop Report](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/110735-over-21-youtube-now-ai-slop-report.html)
- **Mohan, January 2026:** "reducing the spread of low quality, repetitive content" is an explicit goal of existing spam and clickbait systems. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026/)
- **Conflicting claim:** one SEO blog says AI-labeled content is weighted lower in sensitive topics such as finance. It gives no primary source and contradicts YouTube's statements. Treat it as rumor. — [AIR Media-Tech algorithm guide](https://air.io/en/trending/youtube-algorithm-in-2026-month-by-month-changes-that-affect-your-views)
- **Other numbers to ignore:** blogs such as "Creator Visibility Drop by 50%" give unsourced figures. — [rankmybusiness.com.au](https://rankmybusiness.com.au/youtube-ai-update-for-creators/)

### Inferences
- Our long-form stall at 500–1,000 impressions is more consistent with YouTube's normal mechanics than with an AI penalty. YouTube tests a video with a small audience, then expands or stops depending on CTR, retention and satisfaction. Our own analytics (in CLAUDE.md) show:
  - Average long-form retention of 17.6%.
  - More than half of viewers gone by 30–45 seconds.
  - Long-form CTR of 0.1–5.6%.

  Those alone would cap impressions. This is an inference, not proven causation.
- The "AI slop" survey and "Don't recommend channel" are mechanisms through which an obviously synthetic voice or image look could lower reach. That happens only if viewers react negatively to it, not because the video is AI-made.
- Many daily Shorts that look alike (same template, same voice) could also feed "repetitive" signals at channel level. There is no public metric for this.

### Gaps
- No controlled creator experiment was found that compares the same content with an AI voice versus a human voice, or AI images versus stock or original footage, on YouTube reach.
- No official statement on whether "slop" survey responses feed ranking.
- Credible independent data on Browse/Shorts reach of faceless finance channels in 2025–2026 was not found.

## Q3. Does ticking "altered or synthetic content" reduce reach?

### Takeaway
Officially, no. YouTube says so in the help center (2024–2026) and again in the May 27, 2026 label blog post: "a disclosure label alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it's eligible to earn money."
- The penalty applies to not disclosing when disclosure is required.
- Our content (non-photorealistic or illustrative AI images, own-style TTS, AI-assisted scripts) may not even require disclosure. Since May 2026, though, a photorealistic disclosure shows as a visible overlay on Shorts.
- No independent test of viewer-behavior effects was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Help center:** "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." — [Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **What does not require disclosure:**
  - "Creators don't need to disclose non-realistic content that's made with AI, or edits to realistic content that are minor."
  - Production help such as using generative AI "to create or improve a video outline, script."
  - "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs" is listed as minor.
  - What does require disclosure is realistic content, such as making a real person appear to say something they didn't, or altering footage of a real event or place. — [Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **Penalty for not disclosing:** creators who consistently fail to disclose may get a label applied manually and face penalties, "including removal of content or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program." — [Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **May 27, 2026 label changes:**
  - Long-form: the label now appears "directly below the video player, above the description." Shorts: "an overlay on the video itself."
  - YouTube auto-applies labels when significant photorealistic AI is detected and the creator left the question unanswered. Disputes can be filed in Studio.
  - Labels are permanent for YouTube AI tools and for C2PA "fully generative AI" metadata.
  - "a disclosure label alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it's eligible to earn money." — [YouTube Blog: Improving AI labels for viewers and creators](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-shifts-generative-ai-labels-to-spots-viewers-will-actually-see/)
- **Viewer-facing label explainer:** — [Understanding "How this content was made"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836?hl=en)
- **Auto-labeling coverage:** — [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/youtube-is-now-labeling-ai-content-automatically-heres-what-that-means-for-your-channel); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/112570-youtube-cracking-down-undisclosed-ai-videos-but-wont.html)

### Inferences
- There is no algorithmic penalty for the label itself. The label could still lower CTR or "keep watching" indirectly, because viewers see an "altered or synthetic" notice and swipe. This matters most on Shorts, where it is now an on-video overlay. Older viewers seeing a finance video marked synthetic may trust it less.
- Under the help-center criteria, our FLUX images are photorealistic-looking stock-style scenes of houses, documents and people, not real people or real events. Whether they count as "realistic" is a judgment call. A realistic scene that a viewer could mistake for real (for example, an apartment complex) leans toward disclosure.
- Options for the writer to weigh:
  - Keep disclosure on: safe, with no official reach penalty.
  - Disclose only on videos with photorealistic scenes, and use clearly illustrated or graphic styles elsewhere.

  The risk of under-disclosure is a YPP suspension later, which outweighs any CTR gain. FLUX output may also carry C2PA/SynthID-type metadata that triggers auto-labeling anyway (unverified for Cloudflare FLUX).
- An A/B test is possible: compare Shorts "keep watching" percentage for disclosed and undisclosed videos with otherwise similar content. Ethically and policy-wise, only non-realistic content should be left undisclosed.

### Gaps
- No creator A/B test or independent data on the label's effect on CTR or retention was found.
- Unverified whether Cloudflare Workers AI FLUX outputs embed C2PA metadata that would trigger automatic labels.

## Q4. TTS vs human narration: retention and engagement evidence, especially for older audiences. Should we switch to the owner's voice or a professional Korean TTS?

### Takeaway
No credible public study measures YouTube retention for AI versus human narration.
- The best evidence says voice quality and naturalness matter more than AI versus human.
- Trust surveys consistently favor human voices, and "artificial-sounding narration" triggers rejection.
- Policy-wise, TTS is not prohibited. What fails is TTS reading non-original material or templated slideshows.
- For a 45+ Korean audience watching tax and pension advice, a human or highly natural voice is a reasonable, low-regret test. The case for it is trust and the "authenticity" signal, not a policy requirement.

### Cited Findings
- **TechSmith test (vendor study, same instructional video, different voices):** quiz accuracy was high-quality AI 71%, low-quality AI 69%, low-quality human 65%, high-quality human 61%. Voice quality mattered more than AI versus human. — [TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/ai-voices-avatars-in-training-videos/)
- **PaperTok (arXiv 2026), qualitative study of AI short-form science videos:** human presence was the primary credibility signal. Artificial-sounding narration triggered instant rejection for some participants. — [arXiv 2601.18218](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18218)
- **Audacy survey (1,120 US respondents, cited by a voice-talent vendor):** 55% trust a human voice versus 23% for AI-generated content. — [RealVoTalent](https://www.realvotalent.com/ai-vs-human-voiceover) (secondary and vendor-interested)
- **EEG study of Chinese newscasts:** human-voice news produced greater cognitive engagement than AI voice. — [PMC10701901](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10701901/)
- **Policy on TTS:**
  - Vendor and explainer pages say a plain TTS voiceover over a generic AI presentation is not enough for YPP. Original value is required. — [Pictory](https://pictory.ai/blog/ai-voices-and-youtube-monetization-pros-cons-and-things-to-be-aware-of-part-1); [Storyblocks](https://www.storyblocks.com/resources/blog/faceless-youtube-channel)
  - Korean press reports that TTS is viewed as a productivity tool, not banned. Reading news articles verbatim with AI is what gets restricted. — [Daum/관련 보도](https://v.daum.net/v/BqAm9XnQNR); [Carat](https://carat.im/blog/youtube-ai-revenue-policy-analysis)
- **YouTube's own help page** treats "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs" as minor, needing no disclosure. — [Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **Unreliable sources:** Alibaba "product insights" pages cite unverifiable TubeBuddy and "internal YouTube analytics" numbers. Do not use them. — [Alibaba page](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/ai-generated-voiceovers-for-youtube-shorts-do-they-boost-retention-or-make-viewers-skip-faster.html)

### Inferences
- **Our own history** (CLAUDE.md, 2026-09-20 analysis) is the most relevant evidence. After the switch to the cloned and Supertonic voice (8/30), watch ratio fell from 80% to 54%, likes per view fell about 3x, and subscriber conversion fell about 23x. That decline is confounded by format changes, but it is consistent with the trust literature. The audience is 92–96% aged 45+, the group where unnatural Korean prosody and number pronunciation (amounts, dates, percentages) is likely most noticeable.
- **Options, ranked by expected authenticity gain per cost:**
  1. The owner's own voice for long-form (two per week is feasible), and at least for hooks.
  2. Professional Korean TTS (Typecast, ElevenLabs Korean, CLOVA Voice/Dubbing) with natural prosody.
  3. Keep Supertonic but fix prosody and number reading.

  The owner's voice also strengthens the "creator's judgment" signal that both YouTube's policy text and Korean coverage emphasize.
- A human voice is not required by policy. Original analysis, varied structure and educational value are what policy requires.

### Gaps
- No Korean-language or older-audience study of TTS versus human narration on YouTube was found.
- No creator A/B data specific to Typecast, ElevenLabs or CLOVA versus a human voice on YouTube retention.

## Q5. What do successful faceless finance channels do to stay "authentic" in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Across YouTube's "allowed" examples and coverage, authentic AI-assisted channels have a few things in common:
- Each video's substance is materially different: original research, data, and the creator's own interpretation.
- There is a creator-driven narrative or commentary, not slideshow narration.
- Formats vary across videos, not one template.
- No fake human expert persona.
- There is a visible human element (own voice, occasional face or hands, personal takes, community replies).

Evidence on specific successful channels is thin and mostly from blogs.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube's allowed and value criteria:** "the substance of each video should be materially varied and deliver creative, educational, or other value". The same intro and outro is fine if the bulk differs. AI can be used "to build unique characters, narratives, scripts, or backgrounds." — [YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Not allowed:** "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value." — [YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Korean reporting:** AI-assisted videos remain monetizable when they contain the creator's commentary, editing, and original structure or storytelling. — [Newsis 2026-07-26](https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISX20260726_0003723647)
- **Practices reported for faceless creators:** some add on-camera hosts. Niche educational faceless channels reportedly held up better than broad-topic ones (anecdotal). — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- **Explainer blog contrast:** an AI voiceover narrating a well-researched personal-finance explainer is fine, while an AI avatar acting as a credentialed adviser is not. — [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/); see also [Revid](https://www.revid.ai/youtube-ai-monetization-policy), [ScaleLab](https://scalelab.com/en/youtube-monetization-policy-updates)
- **Mohan:** AI "a tool for expression, not a replacement." — [YouTube Blog 2026 letter](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026/)
- **Marketer guidance on competing amid slop:** emphasizes distinctive human perspective and brand trust. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtubes-ai-slop-problem-and-how-marketers-can-compete/567297/)
- **Benchmarks from our own notes** (CLAUDE.md, not external): 경제해적단 (Economy Pirates) uses AI-assisted visuals, and its long-form runs 11–22 min. Its winning topics were broad money topics such as exchange rates, rates, ETFs and ISA, not narrow real estate.

### Inferences
Practical "clearly authentic" changes for our channel, in rough priority order:
1. **Human voice on long-form:** the owner's own voice, or at least an owner-recorded intro and outro with first-person framing ("제가 직접 계산해 보니…").
2. **Visible original analysis in every video:** calculations on real cases, official-source screenshots with citations, "what this means for a 60-year-old one-home owner." This beats generic AI scene images.
3. **Break the daily Shorts template:** rotate three or four formats (Q&A with a viewer comment, number breakdown, myth vs fact, news reaction). Vary visuals beyond FLUX stills: charts, document crops, the creator's hands or screen recording.
4. **Never present the TTS narrator as a credentialed expert persona** (AI Personas rule). State sources, and add a "not tax advice, check with 세무사" note.
5. **Reduce fully generic photorealistic AI scenes**, which also cuts the label-trigger area. Favor data graphics the channel designs itself.
6. **Reply to comments and pin community answers.** This is an engagement and satisfaction signal and a visible human presence. It is an inference; no source quantifies it.

### Gaps
- No verified case study of a Korean faceless finance or real-estate channel that recovered reach after switching to a human voice or a new format.
- No source quantifies how much "human presence" (face, voice) changes recommendation reach as opposed to monetization outcomes.
