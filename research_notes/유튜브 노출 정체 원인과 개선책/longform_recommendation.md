# How YouTube Recommends New Long-Form Videos on Small Channels (2025–2026), and Why Impressions Stall

Research date: 2026-11-28. Case: Korean channel, 253 subscribers, 91% of views from the Shorts feed. Long-form videos (15–17 min) get about 500–1,000 impressions each, CTR 2.4–4.5%, AVD about 2 min (12–23% viewed), Browse 75–82% of views, 15–36 unique viewers per video.

Source-quality labels used below: **[OFFICIAL]** = YouTube Help, YouTube Blog, or a YouTube employee on the record (Creator Insider). **[SECONDARY-OFFICIAL]** = press or blog coverage of an official interview (paraphrase, not a primary transcript). **[INDUSTRY]** = vendor or creator data or opinion. Many marketing blogs carry inconsistent dates or quotes that cannot be traced; those are flagged.

---

## 1. How does YouTube decide how many Browse/Suggested impressions a new long-form video gets, and what expands or stops them?

### Takeaway
YouTube says it does not "push" a video to a fixed test pool. Each viewer's feed is ranked per session, and impressions continue only while the viewers shown the video respond well: they click, they watch a meaningful absolute amount of time (for long videos), and they are satisfied (surveys, likes, not "Not interested"). Good CTR with very low watch time gives the ranker little reason to keep showing the video. YouTube also says impressions plateau when the interested pool for a topic runs out, or when the topic is crowded by established channels.

### Cited Findings
- **Pull, not push.** Todd Beaupré (Senior Director, Growth & Discovery) said recommendation "isn't so much about pushing it out as much as it's pulling," and that the homepage goal is to give each viewer "the best content that is going to make [them] happy today." [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, SEJ, Jan 27 2025, reporting a Rene Ritchie interview] — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- **Per-viewer ranking.** Beaupré: "each viewer gets a different ranking." Ranking is computed per viewer per session. Viewers with more history on a channel or topic rank its videos higher, but early impressions also reach new viewers ("we see diverse audiences"). The system learns separately for subscriber and non-subscriber cohorts, so a video can underperform with subscribers and still do well with others. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Creator Insider interview, Sept 1 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- **Continuous learning, not a one-shot test.** Beaupré said decisions are not based on initial analytics alone. The system learns from a continuous flow of impressions, and no fixed testing window was given. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Sept 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- **Each video is judged on viewer response.** "The system evaluates each piece of content individually." "What matters is how viewers respond to each video when it's recommended to them." Long-term channel performance can suffer if viewers repeatedly stop watching a channel's videos when they are recommended, "rather than a direct algorithmic penalty." [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Help: Good to know about recommendations](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651)
- **No channel-wide score.** Rene Ritchie: "There's no sort of like channelwide score in the algorithm." Outcomes depend on the audience shown each video. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Creator Insider, Nov 24 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/off-topic-videos-can-draw-10-views-or-10-million-youtube-tells-creators/)
- **Absolute watch time matters for long videos.** "relative watch time is more important for short videos and absolute watch time is more important for longer videos." "The system learns how much of the video the viewer watches and if they're satisfied." [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube performance FAQ & Troubleshooting](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **Ranking signals.** Videos are "ranked based on their performance and relevance to your audience." Inputs include what viewers watch, skip, search for, like or dislike, and mark "Not interested." [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube performance FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **Satisfaction and "valued watch time."** YouTube measures "valued watchtime" (time spent on videos viewers consider worthwhile) through surveys that ask viewers to rate videos from 1 to 5 stars. [OFFICIAL, YouTube Blog, 2021, older but still the canonical statement] — [On YouTube's recommendation system](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- **Satisfaction signals in 2025.** Beaupré: YouTube "introduced this concept of satisfaction." Signals include "likes, dislikes, these survey responses." [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Jan 2025] — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/). Another quote is attributed to Beaupré: adding direct-feedback signals "actually leads to people coming back to YouTube more in the long run." It came from search-result snippets and I did not verify it in a primary transcript. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-algorithm/), [The Creators Index](https://thecreatorsindex.com/platform/youtube-updates/youtube-explains-how-its-recommendation-algorithm-actually-works)
- **Caution on "satisfaction replaced watch time" stories.** Blogs claiming a named 2025 or February 2026 switch in which surveys formally outweigh watch time have no YouTube document behind them. — [gleam.fit analysis](https://gleam.fit/blog/youtube-algorithm-satisfaction-beats-watch-time); compare the stronger claims in [dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/youtube-algorithm-2025-how-to-get-your-videos-recommended) and [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/understanding-youtube-algorithm/) [INDUSTRY]
- **Why impressions plateau (official, directly on point).** "Even with excellent CTR and Average View Duration (AVD), your video's overall impressions may still plateau." For hyper-specific topics, the platform "will eventually run out of" interested viewers. Popular topics compete with "many other highly-established channels." "Early high CTR... often comes from your most loyal core audience," and "Once the platform attempts to push it to a broader audience, the CTR performance may drop significantly." [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Help: Decoding CTR & impressions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369?hl=en)
- **CTR falling as reach grows is normal.** "As your content reaches a wider audience and gains more impressions, it is possible that your CTR will decrease." A drop from 9% to 3.5% "is a sign of success." Homepage surfaces "typically yield a high volume of impressions but often a lower CTR." [OFFICIAL] — [Decoding CTR & impressions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369?hl=en)
- **Don't judge CTR or impressions in isolation.** "it's important not to view either in isolation." [OFFICIAL] — [same page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369?hl=en)
- **LLM-based content understanding.** Beaupré said YouTube is moving from "memorizing that this video tends to be good with this type of viewer" toward understanding video content with LLM technology. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Jan 2025] — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- **Context signals.** Time of day and device are used to learn whether different content appeals in different contexts. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL] — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- **Test & Compare winners are chosen by watch time share, not CTR**, which shows YouTube values watch per impression over clicks. [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Help: Test & compare thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714). Title A/B testing (Dec 2025) also picks winners by watch time per impression. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/), [SEJ: title A/B testing global rollout](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators/562571/)
- **Industry diagnosis.** If Browse and Suggested impressions appear and then stop, the usual small-channel cause is weak retention after the click. Videos that never get Browse or Suggested impressions may never have entered the loop. [INDUSTRY, creator blogs, not authoritative] — [VidCRM](https://vidcrm.app/blog/not-getting-impressions-on-youtube), [Nextechads](https://nextechads.com/why-did-my-long-form-views-suddenly-drop-and-is-youtube-prioritizing-shorts-in-2026/), [David Simpson Substack](https://davidsimpson.substack.com/p/why-the-algorithm-didnt-abandon-your)

### Inferences
- In our case CTR (2.4–4.5%) sits inside YouTube's "half of channels" 2–10% band, so packaging is not the bottleneck. The weak signal is about 2 minutes of absolute watch time on a 15–17 minute video, and YouTube says absolute watch time matters most for long videos. A Browse viewer who clicks and leaves after about 2 minutes is likely a weak or negative satisfaction signal, so the ranker stops putting the video on more homepages. That matches "500–1,000 impressions then stop."
- 15–36 unique viewers per video is far too small a sample for the system to find a responsive audience segment. Each new long-form starts with almost no channel-level long-form viewing history to match against. Most watch history the channel has generated is in the Shorts feed (see section 2).
- Browse at 75–82% means YouTube did try the videos on homepages. The problem is not invisibility but failure to earn expansion after the test impressions.

### Gaps
- YouTube has never published numeric thresholds (CTR, AVD, or survey score) that trigger expansion. Any blog giving such thresholds is speculating.
- I could not get a primary transcript of the Sept 2026 or Jan 2025 Beaupré interviews. Quotes are from SEJ and PPC Land coverage.
- I found no official statement on how much the satisfaction-survey weight is relative to watch time.

## 2. Does YouTube separate Shorts audiences from long-form recommendations? Does a Shorts-heavy channel hurt long-form reach?

### Takeaway
YouTube officially says it runs separate recommendation systems for Shorts and long-form that "understand cross-format viewing," and that it has not seen Shorts hurt long-form performance. But it also concedes that viewers don't always engage across formats. No official data shows Shorts viewers converting to long-form. Practically, a Shorts-grown audience does not hand long-form videos a ready-made audience. Long-form has to find its own viewers on Home and Suggested.

### Cited Findings
- **Separate systems.** Myth: "Shorts Hurt Your Long-Form Content's Performance." YouTube: "we've built separate recommendation systems optimized for each." They "understand cross-format viewing" but "primarily focus on serving the unique viewing patterns of each format... Consequently, we haven't seen evidence of Shorts negatively impacting long-form performance." [OFFICIAL, May 6 2025] — [YouTube Blog: Debunking common myths about YouTube Shorts](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/)
- **Cross-format interest is shared, but engagement may not transfer.** "The algorithm aims to understand a viewer's interest across Shorts, long videos, live streams, and posts." "a viewer that often engages with your long-form videos might see your Shorts recommended." "Experimenting with new content formats, such as Shorts... will not inherently confuse the algorithm." The page notes viewers don't always engage across formats, and that "The Shorts feed... may tune up on the recency of content." [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Help 16559651](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651)
- **YouTube's own segmentation of Shorts viewers (July 2026).** The official blog lists "Shorts-only viewers [who] may never click a 10-minute video," context-dependent viewers, and exploratory viewers who browse Shorts before committing to longer content. It recommends Related Video links plus a CTA in the final 5 seconds, and having the long video deliver the promised hook in its first 5–10 seconds. It provides no conversion data. [OFFICIAL, Jul 14 2026] — [YouTube Blog: How to convert YouTube Shorts views into long-form channel growth](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- **Subscribers ≠ active viewers.** "A channel's subscriber count reflects the number of times people have subscribed." YouTube recommends Unique Viewers as the better measure. [OFFICIAL] — [Help 16559651](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651). "The count doesn't represent the number of viewers who watch your videos." [OFFICIAL] — [Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **Subscribers rarely click anyway.** Beaupré: "probably all your videos have about maybe a 10% or less click-through rate" in the subscribers row. Suggested is the top source for subscribers' viewing; the subscriptions feed is "quite small." Notifications are the only push mechanism. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Sept 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- **Shorts-feed watch hours don't count toward the 4,000-hour YPP threshold**: "any qualified watch hours from Shorts views in the Shorts Feed won't count." [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Help: YPP eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)
- **Counter-evidence (preprint).** Rajendran et al. studied 250 creators with over 100K subscribers and found a significant drop in long-form views after each creator's first Short: an average of 743,589 per channel (p=0.011), and 920,189 for long-form-focused creators. There was no significant drop for Shorts-focused creators. The paper has no control group and is not peer-reviewed. [INDUSTRY/ACADEMIC, Feb 2024] — [arXiv 2402.18208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18208); summarized in [creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-long-form)
- **Vendor correlational data.** AIR Media-Tech analyzed 18,000 channels (Mar 2025–Mar 2026). In fitness, food, business, and education, mixing formats correlated with more subscribers. Long-form-only gaming channels had 85% more subscribers. The best results came at roughly 25–40% Shorts share of uploads, and long-form audiences reportedly thinned once Shorts passed 55% of uploads. [INDUSTRY] — [AIR: Do Shorts help long-form? 18,000 channels](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels), [AIR: Shorts vs long-form 2026](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/should-you-chase-shorts-views-or-double-down-on-long-form-for-channel-growth)
- **Another vendor dataset (799,718 videos, Feb 2025 vs Feb 2026).** Channels posting about 5 Shorts a month averaged 46% more views per long-form video, but at 35 or more Shorts a month the lift disappeared. [INDUSTRY, methodology unclear] — reported via search summary of [ScaleLab](https://scalelab.com/en/youtube-shorts-vs-long-videos-what-works-in-2025) / [revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/youtube-shorts-statistics)
- **Unverified claims to avoid.** "Shorts and long-form fully decoupled since late 2025" — single-source blog claim that contradicts YouTube's "cross-format" framing. — [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/shorts-vs-long-form), [AIR month-by-month 2026](https://air.io/en/trending/youtube-algorithm-in-2026-month-by-month-changes-that-affect-your-views). Widely circulated Ritchie/Sherman/Beaupré quotes ("Shorts won't hurt long-form...") could not be traced to a primary source. — [creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-long-form), [marketmakermgmt](https://marketmakermgmt.com/blog-list2/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-your-channel). Korean blogs make the same unverified "완전 분리" claim, and others give conversion rates without sources ("쇼츠 구독자의 롱폼 전환율 8~12%"). — [viralpulse](https://viralpulse.net/blog/shorts-vs-longform), [money-read](https://www.money-read.com/posts/youtube-shorts-vs-longform/), [visual.center](https://visual.center/posts/36)
- **Korean observation (unverified).** On the mobile home screen, one long-form slot can appear alongside about six Shorts, so long-form gets less homepage space. No measurement method is given. — [visual.center 2026 유튜브 알고리즘의 변화](https://visual.center/posts/36)
- **Separate channel vs same channel.** Ritchie argues each topic or brand means "climbing two different mountains." He recommends an "anchor point where the channel makes sense to an audience" and warns that off-topic uploads mean "rebuilding an audience every time." This talk is about topics, not formats. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Nov 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/off-topic-videos-can-draw-10-views-or-10-million-youtube-tells-creators/)

### Inferences
- With 91% of views in the Shorts feed, almost all of this channel's viewer-affinity data comes from people watching 40-second vertical clips. YouTube says the long-form system "understands cross-format viewing," but the evidence it gives (and its own July 2026 segmentation) suggests many of these viewers are "Shorts-only." So long-form starts nearly cold. This is an inference, not an official mechanism statement.
- The data does not support starting a separate channel for long-form as an automatic fix. YouTube says Shorts don't harm long-form. The bigger lever is making the long-form satisfy the people Home shows it to. A separate channel would also lose the 253 subscribers and the shared topic history. If the channel's long-form keeps failing after retention fixes, a separate long-form channel is a test the user could choose. No 2024–2026 official statement recommends it.
- Topic consistency matters more than format. This project's long-form recently widened to stocks and money (CLAUDE.md, 11/8 decision). By Ritchie's logic, each new topic cluster is a separate audience to rebuild. The channel's own data (stocks CTR 0.1–2.5% vs taxes and pensions 3–5.6%) is consistent with that.

### Gaps
- No official or credible data measures how many Shorts-acquired subscribers watch long-form.
- YouTube gave no official guidance on separate channel vs same channel for Shorts and long-form in 2024–2026. I found only topic-consistency advice.
- I found no Korean-language official YouTube (YouTube Korea) statement on this.

## 3. Is there a "new upload test pool," how long is a video tested, and can it be revived later?

### Takeaway
There is no officially described fixed "test batch." Early impressions go to a mix of channel-affine viewers and "diverse audiences." The video remains eligible indefinitely, and YouTube says evergreen videos can get most of their views a month or more later. Revival happens when a topic becomes relevant again, during binge-watching, or when a new episode in a series lifts older ones.

### Cited Findings
- **Early impressions are mixed.** Viewers with history on the channel or topic rank the video higher, but early impressions also reach diverse new viewers. Subscriber and non-subscriber cohorts are learned separately. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Sept 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- **No fixed window, and late peaks happen.** Some videos peak on day one, while others gain most of their audience a month or more after publishing. Evergreen content stays eligible. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Sept 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/); also summarized in [founderpresence substack](https://founderpresence.substack.com/p/did-the-youtube-algorithm-change) [INDUSTRY]
- **Repeat exposure.** YouTube tested a hard cap ("you wouldn't be recommended a video more than eight times") and found engagement sometimes came on later attempts. One viewer had been shown a creator's uploads 19 times without noticing. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Sept 2026] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- **Revival is official.** "It's common for viewers to start showing more interest in old videos." Reasons include a topic gaining popularity, binge-watching, or a new episode in a series. [OFFICIAL] — [Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en). Beaupré: an event can make "this video relevant again... to a different audience than enjoyed it the first time." [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL, Jan 2025] — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- **Judge over longer windows.** Beaupré: "seasonality can play a role," look at "90 days or more," and "I would encourage you not to worry about it too much" about fluctuations. [SECONDARY-OFFICIAL] — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- **Metadata changes.** "Changing a video's title or thumbnail can lead to a shift in its performance." YouTube says change metadata when CTR is low and impressions are below normal, and "not to change what is already working well." [OFFICIAL] — [Help 16559651](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651)
- **Unlisted first is fine.** "uploading a video as unlisted before making it public should not significantly impact its overall performance." [OFFICIAL] — [Help 16559651](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651)
- **Library size helps new channels.** "For new channels, gaining this credibility and building a community takes time." A larger library of quality videos helps new viewers explore and can make future recommendations more likely. [OFFICIAL] — [Help 16559651](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651)
- **Industry heuristic (unverified).** Suggested impressions within 72 hours predict long-term performance. This is one creator's analysis, not established. — [Creators Toolbox substack](https://creatorstoolbox.substack.com/p/this-metric-can-predict-a-videos) [INDUSTRY]

### Inferences
- The long-form videos are not "dead." They stay eligible. They can be revived when (a) a related news event makes the topic relevant (tax deadlines, policy announcements: this niche is very event-driven), (b) a newer video in the same series pulls viewers back to it, or (c) a newly satisfied audience segment exists to match them to.
- Low view counts in the first week are not a final verdict, but YouTube gives no evidence that low-retention videos later recover without a change in relevance.

### Gaps
- YouTube does not publish the size of the early exposure pool or how many impressions a new video from a small channel gets before the system decides.
- No official data shows how often old videos actually get revived.

## 4. Realistic benchmarks for impressions, CTR, and AVD on small channels

### Takeaway
The only official benchmark is CTR: half of channels and videos sit between 2% and 10%. There is no official AVD or retention benchmark. Third-party ranges put typical average-percentage-viewed for 10–20+ minute videos at about 35–50%, with a platform-wide average near 24% in one vendor dataset. On those numbers, 12–23% on 15–17 minute videos is below typical, while CTR is normal.

### Cited Findings
- **Official CTR range.** Half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%. New channels, videos less than a week old, and videos with fewer than 100 views can show a wider range. Small videos may show higher CTR because their audience is narrower and more loyal. [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Help: Impressions & CTR FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)
- **No official impression benchmarks.** The 9% and 3.5% figures on the official page are illustrations. The only comparison point given is your own channel's past videos. [OFFICIAL] — [Decoding CTR & impressions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369?hl=en)
- **Retention ranges (third-party, they disagree):**
  - 10–20 min: 40–55% typical, per one source; 35–45% per another. — [prepublish.ai retention benchmarks](https://prepublish.ai/blog/youtube-retention-benchmarks-2026), [prepublish.ai AVD](https://prepublish.ai/blog/good-average-view-duration-youtube)
  - 15–30 min: 35–45%. — [lenostube](https://www.lenostube.com/en/youtube-audience-retention-average-good-and-best-benchmarks/), [influencernotes](https://influencernotes.com/academy/whats-a-good-retention-rate)
  - Retention Rabbit 2025 report (10,000+ videos, 75+ niches): 23.7% average retention, 55% drop-off in the first minute, and only 16.8% of videos above 50%. [INDUSTRY] — cited in [tubelab](https://tubelab.net/blog/youtube-audience-retention) / [prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/blog/youtube-retention-benchmarks-2026)
  - Caveat: many length-bracket tables trace back to a 2017 Backlinko ranking-correlation study, not measured retention. — [prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-retention-guide)
- **New vs returning viewers.** YouTube counts a viewer as "new" if they haven't watched the channel in over a year (or watch privately or deleted their history). A channel with mostly new viewers has viewers who "are less likely to return." Studio can compare retention for new vs returning viewers and subscribers vs non-subscribers, and break new and returning viewers down by format. [OFFICIAL] — [Understand returning & new viewers](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996), [Understand your audience](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416), [Measure key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)
- **Industry claim on Browse (unverified methodology).** A weekly channel with 55–60% midpoint retention earns more Browse impressions per upload than a daily channel at 20–25%. [INDUSTRY] — surfaced via [AIR Media-Tech promotion audits](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/how-to-promote-a-youtube-channel-in-2026-tactics-from-3000-audits)

### Inferences
- Against these ranges, the channel's long-form CTR (2.4–4.5%) is normal for homepage-heavy traffic. Homepage CTR is lower by design, per YouTube.
- AVD of about 2 minutes on 15–17 minutes (12–23%) falls at or below the platform-wide vendor average of 23.7%, and well below the 35–45% range commonly cited for this length. The channel's own data (CLAUDE.md, 11/5: more than half of viewers leave within 30–45 seconds) matches the industry "first-minute drop-off" pattern. This is the most likely reason expansion stops.
- 500–1,000 impressions per long-form video on a 253-subscriber channel is not unusual, but I found no credible published benchmark for small-channel impressions.

### Gaps
- No official or credible public dataset gives impression counts for new long-form videos on channels under 1,000 subscribers.
- No Korean-language benchmark data for the finance/real-estate niche was found.

## 5. Concrete levers to grow long-form impressions for a small channel (with evidence)

### Takeaway
The officially supported levers all aim at the same thing: earn more satisfied watch time from the people Home already shows the video to. Deliver the promised value in the first seconds, keep topics consistent around an anchor, build series and playlists so one video leads to the next (feeding Suggested and revival), connect each Short to its matching long-form, and A/B test packaging by watch time share. Upload frequency itself is not causal, per YouTube.

### Cited Findings
- **Fix the opening.** YouTube's own guide says the long video's first 5–10 seconds should deliver the hook the Short promised: skip intros, logos, and unrelated updates, "since viewers who must search for the content leave quickly." [OFFICIAL, Jul 2026] — [YouTube Blog related-videos guide](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- **Use retention analytics and adjust length.** YouTube recommends paying attention to audience retention to see how long viewers stay, and adjusting content length accordingly. [OFFICIAL] — [Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **Related Video links on Shorts plus a CTA in the last 5 seconds.** Match the link to the Short's intent. The target can be changed later, so old Shorts can point to new long-form. [OFFICIAL, no effect-size data] — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/). Third-party estimates of click-through are very low (about 0.05% to under 1%), and a pinned comment link may convert at 1–3%. [INDUSTRY, unverified] — [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-shorts-related-video-manager-strategy/), [joyspace](https://joyspace.ai/related-video-bridge-funnel-views), [subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/drive-traffic-shorts-to-long-videos), [ContentFries](https://www.contentfries.com/blog/youtube-shorts-promote-long-form-videos)
- **Dual or vertical live streams** appear in the Shorts feed (a "Watch Live" entry point), which is another bridge. [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- **Topic anchor and consistency.** Ritchie says to keep an anchor point (personality, format, perspective, or mission). He asks whether "could this video exist on other channels?" ### Inferences (prioritized for this channel)
1. **Retention first.** The biggest gap versus benchmarks is AVD. Goal: move 15–17 minute videos from about 2 minutes toward 4–6+ minutes of absolute watch time. Ways to get there: open with the payoff the thumbnail promised (YouTube says within 5–10 seconds), cut the slow 30–45 second intro (the channel's own retention data), and consider shorter long-form (8–12 minutes). A shorter video at the same absolute minutes gives a higher percentage, and YouTube itself says to adjust length to retention. The channel's 11/6 decision to target 18–22 minutes (copying a 640k-subscriber benchmark channel) runs against this evidence for a 253-subscriber channel. This is an inference worth flagging to the user.
2. **Stay on the anchor topic.** The channel's own data shows tax, pension, and reconstruction topics get 3–5.6% CTR versus 0.1–2.5% for stocks. Ritchie's "rebuilding an audience every time" warning suggests concentrating long-form on taxes, policy, and pensions until a satisfied audience segment exists.
3. **Series and playlists.** Make each long-form part of a named series (e.g. a year-end tax series), and point end screens and the description to the next video. This builds the binge and Suggested paths that YouTube lists as revival triggers.
4. **Shorts → long-form bridge.** Set a Related Video link on every derived Short, add a spoken CTA in the last 5 seconds, and make the long-form deliver that exact Short's promise in its first 10 seconds. Expect low click rates (well under 1%). The value is in getting the long-form watched by viewers who actually chose it, which may produce stronger satisfaction signals than cold Home viewers.
5. **Time videos to events.** The niche is deadline- and policy-driven, and revival is officially linked to topics "becoming relevant again." Publish ahead of known dates (filing deadlines, tax bill dates), and refresh titles and thumbnails of older related long-form when the topic resurfaces. Only change metadata when CTR and impressions are below normal, per YouTube.
6. **Test & Compare on every long-form.** It optimizes for watch time share, which matches the stall mechanism. Let tests run up to 2 weeks.
7. **Judge on 90-day windows**, not first-week impressions, per Beaupré.
8. **Don't add cadence for its own sake.** YouTube says frequency isn't causal. Quality per video is the lever.
